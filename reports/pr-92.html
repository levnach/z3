In one paragraph

Two distinct assertions over the same terms, written in different orders, can now be certified in Lean. The typical input asserts (distinct c0 ... c11) and the negation of the same distinct with its arguments reversed. Z3's rewriter sorts a distinct's arguments, so it sees the conflict at once. The exported certificate was another story: its Lean check ran out of heartbeats from 12 Int arguments. The fix reads each distinct as the conjunction of its pairwise disequalities. It then links each pair a = b to its twin b = a by the symmetry of equality. With those links, propagation alone closes the CNF lemma that ties the two distincts. Every new step is checked by Lean, and no new axiom is involved.

Background

Before this PR, the Lean reconstructor could split a distinct into its pairs only in one case. The clause log had to hold an equality (= a b) in the distinct's own argument order. A pair logged with swapped sides did not count, and neither did a second distinct over the same terms. Such a distinct stayed one opaque atom of the lemma. The lemma then fell back to the omega and grind tactics over every unfolded pair. That fallback timed out at 12 Int terms and again at 33. This PR serves Milestone 3 of the project plan, "Equality and linear arithmetic". It closes two backlog items, from iterations 53 and 90.

What changed

Think of two guest lists, one sorted A to Z and one sorted Z to A. Before, Lean compared them as two sealed envelopes. Now it opens both and pairs each name with its twin.

  • Exporter: a distinct now splits into its pairs when a pair is logged with swapped sides, as (= b a).
  • Exporter: a distinct also splits into its pairs when another distinct ranges over the same terms, in any order.
  • Exporter: each such pair is tied to its swapped twin by one eq_comm step, a fact from Lean's core library.
  • Tests: reversed distincts of 5, 12, and 33 terms over Int, Real, and an uninterpreted sort are now checked, with the slow fallback removed.
  • Docs: the project plan records the measured cost at 60 terms and the known limits of the change.

The main theorem says the two assertions together imply False, for every choice of Int values.

Review

The reviewer approved the change. There were no blocker or major findings.

  • Minor: cost above 33 terms was untested. The reviewer built a reversed distinct of 60 Int terms. It passed, but took about 32 seconds. Time grew much faster than the number of bridges, so about 100 terms might hit the heartbeat limit. This is a speed issue, not a soundness issue. Such inputs already failed before the PR. Resolution: the measurement is now recorded in the project plan. No size cap was added.
  • Minor: the trigger is global, not per clause. A distinct now splits into pairs in every lemma once a twin distinct exists anywhere in the certificate. That can hand the prover many pairs with nothing to bridge to. The reviewer tried two probes to provoke this. Both gave output identical to the old exporter. Resolution: not fixed. It is documented and listed as a follow-up.
  • Nit: a test assertion depended on log trimming. One check counted how many pair equalities Z3's trimmed log happens to keep. A harmless change to trimming could have broken it. Resolution: the assertion was removed. The test still checks the total count and one bridge per pair.

Risks and open questions

  • The prover now sees pairs in more lemmas. Its work is bounded, and a failed attempt falls back to the old proof.
  • Very wide reordered distincts, around 100 terms, may still time out.
  • Each bridge relies on Lean unfolding a definition during unification. If that failed, Lean would reject the attempt. It could not accept a wrong proof.
  • GitHub CI results for the merged commit are not known to this report.

What comes next

  • Split a distinct for a twin only when the twin appears in the same clause.
  • Bridge swapped pairs outside CNF lemmas too.
  • Rescan benchmarks with wide distincts, an open item from iteration 90.

Glossary

atom
A formula with no Boolean connective inside, treated as one unit by propositional reasoning. Example: c0 = c1.
axiom
A statement Lean accepts without proof. A trustworthy certificate adds none.
certificate
The file Z3 exports that records the steps of its proof, for an outside checker to replay.
clause
A disjunction of literals. Example: (or (not p) q).
clause log
Z3's record of the clauses its SAT core learned or used, from which the certificate is built.
CNF lemma
A clause that holds by Boolean structure alone, used to turn formulas into clause form. Here it says the two distincts cannot both hold as asserted.
conjunction
An "and" of several formulas. Each part is called a conjunct.
disequality
A statement that two terms differ. Example: (not (= a b)).
distinct
The SMT-LIB operator saying all its arguments differ. (distinct a b c) means a≠b, a≠c, and b≠c.
grind
A general Lean tactic that combines case splits, congruence, and arithmetic.
heartbeat
Lean's unit of work. A proof step that uses more than the set limit is stopped with a timeout error.
literal
An atom or its negation. Example: p or (not p).
negation
The "not" of a formula. It is true exactly when the formula is false.
omega
A Lean tactic that decides linear arithmetic over Int and Nat.
propagation
Deriving new facts from known ones step by step, such as "a and b holds, so a holds". The reconstructor uses it to prove CNF lemmas cheaply.
refutation
A proof that a set of assertions leads to False.
rewriter
The part of Z3 that simplifies terms before solving, for example by sorting arguments.
sort
The type of a value in SMT-LIB, such as Int, Real, or Bool.
symmetry of equality
The rule that a = b holds exactly when b = a holds. In Lean it is the lemma eq_comm.
tactic
A Lean command that builds part of a proof automatically.
uninterpreted sort
A sort declared with no built-in meaning, only equality. Example: (declare-sort U 0).
