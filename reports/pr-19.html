In one paragraph

Real division axioms now survive export when their hints contain a redundant true literal. Gate clauses also work when an if-then-else condition contains negation and Z3 simplifies its guard. The resulting refutations still require checked proofs for every recorded inference. No new trusted assumptions are introduced.

Background

The pipeline already supported linear real arithmetic and uninterpreted functions through both proof cores. Larger random examples exposed valid native shapes that the adapter rejected. Even an inference unnecessary for the final contradiction can appear in a certificate and must pass validation. This change closes those completeness gaps without adding another theory fragment.

What changed

  • Real division hints retain their meaningful arithmetic evidence after redundant true literals are removed.
  • Removing a weighted literal also removes its matching coefficient, keeping the remaining pairs aligned.
  • Negated conditions are proved by splitting on their innermost proposition and introducing the required negations.
  • Nested conditions and reversed guards now have dedicated positive and negative regressions.

Worked example

This input is deliberately small. Its ground division term causes the native arithmetic solver to log a division axiom, despite the immediate contradiction in the first assertion.

(set-logic QF_LRA)
(declare-const x Real)
(declare-const y Real)
(declare-const z Real)
(assert (< x x))
(assert (or (< y z) (< (/ 1.0 2.0) 3.0)))
(check-sat)

Review

The interrupted tests referenced a missing fixture. Completing it exposed another test issue: the gate selector included ordinary equality tautologies. Those clauses legitimately need different proof witnesses. Restricting the selector fixed the assertion without weakening the existing congruence tests. Reversed-guard rejection and nested-condition checks were added before approval. No review findings remain open.

Risks and open questions

Only logical true is removed from contradictory hint sets. Otherwise-empty arithmetic hints remain errors. Gate proofs introduce negations constructively. Unsupported shapes still fail explicitly, and failed checking never publishes a certified proof.

What comes next

A separately reproduced native arithmetic-checker strictness issue belongs in the next iteration. It is independent of this adapter repair and of Lean's proof obligations.

Glossary

Axiom
A rule of the input theory whose instance must still be justified in the reconstructed proof.
Coefficient
A multiplier attached to an arithmetic premise, such as the 2 in twice an inequality.
Gate clause
A clause connecting a compound expression to its parts, such as an if-then-else term and its selected branch.
Hint
Native evidence suggesting how an inference can be checked, not permission to trust the inference.
Literal
An atomic proposition or its negation, including the Boolean constants.
Negation
The logical operation saying that a proposition does not hold.
Refutation
A proof deriving contradiction from the input assertions.
Uninterpreted function
A function constrained by the input and by equal arguments having equal results.
