Give normalized-rewrite links a capped budget of their own in Lean

Pull request #118 on levnach/z3 · State: OPEN · Opened 2026-11-11T11:56:02Z · Loop iteration 117

This pull request is open and not merged. The change under review is commit 3c8cb88. It contains two commits on top of ac7f8bc.

In one paragraph

Some lemmas in the generated Lean file prove that a normalized rewrite is an equivalence. They are proved by a small propositional prover with a fixed step budget. That prover first builds links between parts of the two sides that differ only by flattening. On one large QF_LRA benchmark, the 481 links used up the whole budget. The search over the two sides then never ran. The lemma fell back to the grind tactic, which failed. This PR gives each link a small allowance of its own, capped at the size of the normal budget. The benchmark tta_startup/simple_startup_15nodes now verifies in Lean. No new axiom or trust is added. Every step is still a have that the Lean kernel checks.

Background

Before this PR, the previous iteration let the same benchmark pass its CNF lemma. The file still failed at one normalized rewrite. Its left side is a left-nested tree of binary conjunctions and disjunctions over 480 atoms. Its right side is the same formula after the rewriter flattened it. The structural prover gave up, and the fallback could not prove it either. The pipeline could already handle smaller rewrites of this shape. It could not handle ones whose links alone cost more than the budget. This work serves milestone 3 of the plan, linear arithmetic, checked against the z3test benchmark sample.

What changed

  • A normalized rewrite with many links now keeps its full search budget, so the prover can finish the structural proof instead of handing it to grind.
  • The extra allowance is capped at the normal budget, so a rewrite's proof can grow to at most twice the old size limit.
  • Very large rewrites, such as one with 931 links, still go to grind alone, exactly as before this PR.
  • A new test builds a small rewrite with the same shape and checks it in Lean with the grind fallback removed.
  • The plan document gains a dated section that explains the allowance, the cap, and the measurements.

Think of a hiking trip with one shared food bag. Before, the climb to base camp ate all the food, and nobody could reach the summit. Now base camp gets its own small bag. The summit team still starts with the full shared bag.

Worked example

The real input this PR fixes is 180 KB, far too big to show. So this example uses the PR's own test generator, startup_shaped_rewrite_instance, with 3 nodes instead of 6. The declarations are grouped six to a line. At the default limits, the old and new code already agree on an input this small. The PR's test therefore scales the step limit down to mimic the large case. Here the limit is set to 300 steps instead of 20,000. All output below comes from real runs of the old and new scripts.

(b) Before

The old code gave no error message on this input. Its prover silently ran out of steps while building the 13 links. It then left the whole lemma to grind:

set_option maxHeartbeats 1000000 in
private theorem normalized_rewrite_154 (_atoms : Nat -> Prop) (_atom0 : Prop) ... (_atom17 : Prop)

  by grind (splits := 64)

On this tiny input, Lean accepted that file because grind can prove the lemma. On the real 15-node benchmark, the same situation ended in failure. According to the PR, grind failed on normalized_rewrite_15304 after 156 s at 3.83 GB. I could not find a small input that the old code rejects outright. The difference only turns into a rejection at the large scale, where grind gives up. I did not rerun that 156-second check myself.

Now the lemma has a full structural proof. The 13 links, _s36 to _s48, each say that a nested group equals its flattened copy. The first link, _f19, shows that a ∧ (b ∧ c) and (a ∧ b) ∧ c agree. The last link covers the whole formula, and two short steps finish the equivalence. grind remains only as a fallback.

Review

The reviewer first asked for changes. All three findings were fixed in the second commit.

SeverityFindingResolution
majorThe link allowance had no upper limit.Fixed: capped at the normal budget.
minorThe new test kept the grind fallback during the Lean check.Fixed: checked with the fallback removed.
nitThe plan still described the old size bound.Fixed: the plan now states the doubled bound.

Major. The reviewer scaled the new test's generator up to 30, 35, and 40 nodes. Before the PR, these rewrites went to grind and passed. With the uncapped allowance, they got huge structural proofs instead. Lean became three to five times slower on them. At 40 nodes, the kernel ran out of memory, so an input that used to verify now failed. This mattered because a budget exists to keep proofs inside Lean's limits. The fix caps the allowance at the budget itself. The 30-, 35-, and 40-node files became byte-identical to the old ones again. The 15-node benchmark still passes. A new test pins the cap.

Minor. The new test checked its file with grind still present as a fallback. If a link were broken, grind would quietly prove the lemma anyway. The test would then pass and hide the bug. The reviewer showed by hand that a tampered link is rejected once the fallback is gone. The test now removes the fallback before checking. Its step limit went from 300 to 1,000 so that the 6-node case fits under the cap.

Nit. The plan document still said the structural proof was bounded by the old limits. A reader could have trusted a wrong size estimate. The text now says the bound is up to twice those limits for linked rewrites.

Risks and open questions

  • Soundness is unchanged. No axiom, sorry, or skipped check was added.
  • Some rewrites now get a structural proof of up to about 2 MB, which Lean tries before grind. Such a proof is valid but may check more slowly than grind would. The scan found no such input besides the 15-node benchmark.
  • The 15-node benchmark itself is not pinned by a test. That would need 180 KB of test data in a slow test.
  • The scan could not judge some inputs, such as the Heizmann and most LassoRanker inputs. They hit z3's 60-second limit or the scan's deadline. Other logics with normalized rewrites were not scanned.
  • CI and local gate results are unknown at the time of writing.

What comes next

The PR and the reviewer both suggest making each link cheaper. A link could be proved with a direct re-association term instead of a search. That would cut the per-link cost that made the allowance necessary. It is listed in the backlog.

Glossary

atom
A formula with no Boolean connectives inside, such as x1 <= 5. The Lean file names them _atom0, _atom1, and so on.
axiom
A statement that Lean accepts without proof. Adding a new one would mean trusting something unchecked.
budget (step budget)
A limit on how many proof steps and characters the prover may produce before it gives up.
CNF (conjunctive normal form)
A formula written as an AND of clauses, where each clause is an OR of literals.
conjunction
An AND of formulas, written and in SMT-LIB and And in Lean.
disjunction
An OR of formulas, written or in SMT-LIB and Or in Lean.
equivalence
A statement that two formulas are true in exactly the same cases, written Iff in Lean.
fallback
A second proof method tried only when the first fails. Here, first | structural proof | grind.
flattening
Turning nested binary operators into one operator with many arguments. For example, (and (and a b) c) becomes (and a b c).
grind
A general-purpose automatic Lean tactic. It can prove many lemmas, but it may fail or use much memory on large ones.
have
A Lean step that states a fact and proves it, so later steps can use it.
kernel
The small core of Lean that checks every proof term. It is the part that must be trusted.
left-nested
A chain of binary operators grouped to the left, such as ((a or b) or c) or d.
lemma
A helper theorem proved on the way to the main theorem.
link
In this prover, a proved equivalence between two parts of a rewrite that differ only by flattening or regrouping.
normalized rewrite
A proof step where the rewriter turns an assertion into a simpler but equivalent form. Lean must prove the two forms equivalent.
propositional
About formulas built from atoms with AND, OR, NOT, and IMPLIES only, where the atoms are treated as plain true-or-false values.
QF_LRA
The SMT-LIB logic of quantifier-free linear real arithmetic. Example: (<= (+ x y) 5) over Real variables.
re-association
Regrouping an operator's arguments without changing their order, as in (a ∧ b) ∧ c to a ∧ (b ∧ c).
rewriter
The z3 component that simplifies formulas before solving, for example by flattening.
structural proof
A step-by-step proof that follows the shape of the two formulas, written out as have steps.
tactic
A Lean command that builds part of a proof automatically, such as grind or refine.
