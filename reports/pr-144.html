Prove the facts of a cnf lemma apart from one another

Merged on 2026-11-17T12:05:20Z as commit 03f517a12.

In one paragraph

To prove a cnf lemma in Lean, the reconstructor first states helper facts: bridges and theory clauses. Each fact used to be a step of its own, and its tactic read every earlier fact as a hypothesis. With hundreds of facts the work grew with the square of their number, and the lemma ran out of heartbeats. This PR states all the facts as the binders of one function, with their proofs as its arguments. Each proof is now checked alone. The statements, the tactics, and the refutation are unchanged. A linear real arithmetic benchmark whose certificate puts 694 facts in one lemma is certified again.

Background

Before this PR the pipeline already proved these lemmas, as long as they stayed small. The benchmark verified on 2026-11-13. Then its certificate changed, and its one cnf lemma grew from 311 facts to 694. Lean stopped the lemma at its heartbeat limit, although the proofs take under a second when checked apart. The PR serves milestone 3 of the plan, equality and linear arithmetic, as scale work.

What changed

An analogy: a grader marks 694 answers. Before, the grader re-read every earlier answer before marking the next one. Now each answer is marked on its own sheet.

  • Exporter, layout. The checking time of a cnf lemma now grows with the number of its facts, not with the square.
  • Exporter, guard. A fact that Lean cannot prove still fails the lemma's first attempt, so the fallback (a second proof of the same lemma) is tried.
  • Tests. New tests generate lemmas with hundreds of facts and tamper with facts, expecting a fallback or a rejection.
  • Benchmark data. The 15-node startup benchmark is now test data for a slow-tier test.

No C++ or Lean source changed, and no limit moved.

Review

Verdict: approve. The reviewer found no blocker and no major issue.

  • Minor, fixed. The reviewer wrote small Lean files with a fact that cannot be proved. In the old layout the lemma then moved on to its fallback. In the new layout, when the tactic failed under a bullet or the proof stopped early, Lean's error recovery logged the error and admitted the fact. The fallback was never tried, so Lean rejected a certificate the old layout verified. No wrong proof was accepted, and today's proofs do not reach the case. The fix is the guard, first | ((tactics); done) | fail, with a new Lean test.
  • Nit, documented. The exporter drops a first attempt above a fixed length, and a fact is now 43 characters longer. A lemma near that bound could lose its attempt. No sample lemma did.
  • Two nits, fixed. A code comment used the old name for a fact, and the plan document understated the text growth.

Risks and open questions

  • A fact is no longer proved with earlier facts in view. A proof that needed one would send its lemma to the fallback. None does in the sample or the tests.
  • One fast test pins a heartbeat limit. A Lean toolchain change that shifts heartbeat counts could fail it.
  • The layout was measured up to 1,601 facts in one lemma. Tens of thousands were not tried.
  • Three large files were 1 to 3% slower after the change. That was not looked into.
  • Unknown: which change took the benchmark's bridges from 245 to 665.

What comes next

  • The fallback proof still states its own helper facts one after another. They are the first candidate for the same layout and guard.
  • A refutation names few of the facts its lemma states: 32 of 694 in the benchmark. Stating only those would shorten the lemmas.
  • A variant of the generated family, with all comparisons in one clause, fails at 400 literals with both exporters.

Glossary

assertion
A formula the input states as true, written (assert ...) in SMT-LIB.
benchmark
A standard input file used to test and compare solvers. Here: a file of the SMT-LIB collection.
binder
The named parameter of a function, with its type. In fun (h : P) => ... the binder is h : P, and an argument for it must be a proof of P.
Boolean
A value that is either true or false. Its sort is written Bool.
bridge
In this project, a fact saying that two comparisons describe one constraint. Example: 1 <= x holds exactly when x < 1 does not.
certificate
The record of proof steps the solver writes out, so that another program can check the result.
clause
A disjunction of literals, such as b or 1 <= x.
cnf lemma
A certificate step claiming that a clause follows from an assertion by its Boolean structure alone, as when the assertion is put into conjunctive normal form. The reconstructor must prove that claim in Lean.
conjunct
One of the formulas joined by and in a conjunction.
conjunctive normal form (CNF)
A formula written as a conjunction of clauses.
error recovery
Lean's habit of logging an error in one part of a proof and continuing, so that it can report several errors at once. The file is still rejected.
goal
The statement a proof still has to establish at a given point.
heartbeat
Lean's unit for counting the work spent on one declaration. A declaration that passes its heartbeat limit is stopped with a timeout error.
hypothesis
A fact that is assumed or already proved, and available while proving the goal.
linear real arithmetic
The theory of sums and comparisons of real-valued variables with constant coefficients. Its quantifier-free SMT-LIB logic is named QF_LRA.
literal
An atomic formula or its negation, such as x < 1 or not b.
refutation
A proof that a set of formulas is contradictory, ending in False.
sort
The type of a value, such as Int, Real, or Bool.
tactic
A Lean command that builds part of a proof automatically. Example: grind searches for a proof from the hypotheses in view.
theory clause
A clause that is true by the rules of a theory alone, here arithmetic. Example: not (x < 1) or not (2 <= x).
