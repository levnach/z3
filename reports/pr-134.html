Certify Int lemmas of the nonlinear solver by polynomial refutations

Pull request levnach/z3#134 into master · State: MERGED · Date: 2026-11-13T19:16:19Z · Loop iteration 133

Merge commit: 83e70ba42, merged 2026-11-13T19:16:19Z

In one paragraph

This PR certifies clauses that are true because of what a product of terms of sort Int means. One such clause says that m ≥ 2 and n ≥ 3 give m·n ≥ 6. Z3's nonlinear solver uses such clauses as theory lemmas. Until now a refutation that needed one was rejected or failed in Lean. The pipeline now searches for a polynomial refutation of the lemma's literals, and Lean proves the lemma from it. Nothing new is trusted: no axiom is added, and Lean checks the polynomial identity itself. On the nonlinear test corpus, 24 of 78 unsat queries now verify in Lean, up from 14.

Background

Since #133 a product of Int terms is an atom of linear arithmetic, so refutations that use only a product's bounds were already certified. A refutation that used a lemma of the nonlinear solver was still rejected by name. The same reasoning also reached the clause log under other hints, such as cnf and cut, and Lean's tactics could not prove those clauses. The work serves milestone 4 of the plan, “Broader theories and integration”, which names nonlinear arithmetic.

What changed

A polynomial refutation works like a receipt: anyone can add up the lines and see that the total is impossible.

  • Search. You can now ask polynomial_refutation for multipliers that make the denied facts, their pairwise products, and squares add up to −1.
  • Exporter. A solver lemma with such a refutation now enters the certificate, and so does a clause the log only assumed on that solver's word.
  • Exporter. When trimming removed unit clauses that unit propagation needs under a lemma, the untrimmed log is replayed, with a warning.
  • Reconstructor. Lean now receives a proof written from the refutation, like a Farkas lemma proof with products among the summands.
  • Tests. A new test module covers the search, 18 documented inputs, pinned logs, and tampered certificates.

Cut, bound, and cnf lemmas over Int products get the same proof. A solver lemma without a refutation is still rejected.

Review

The reviewer approved, with no blocker and no major finding.

  • Minor, fixed: the gain was invisible to another harness. The reviewer ran the new inputs through z3test's harness, which measures progress. It skipped the fallback to the untrimmed log, so four of the ten newly certified corpus queries failed there. The fallback moved into build_certificate, which the harness calls.
  • Minor, documented, not changed: five rejections became Lean failures. The exporter used to reject five inputs with a clear message. They are now exported and fail in Lean on another step. Nothing unsound is accepted, but the gap shows up later. No rule was found that rejects them early without rejecting inputs that verify, so the docs name them.
  • Minor, fixed: a test that could not fail. A test named “stays rejected” never reached its rejection branch, so a wrong certification would have passed it. It now pins each input's hints and proof count, and a new test asserts that Lean rejects both inputs.
  • Four nits and one suggestion, all applied. A comment and the docs were corrected. The second replay now shares the first one's deadline and warns. A cut or cnf lemma keeps its older proofs when the search raises an error.

Risks and open questions

  • The exporter and the reconstructor each run the search and could disagree at the size limit. This was not observed.
  • The cost of the search on large nonlinear logs was not measured.
  • Three random seeds seem to verify through more compact certificates, not a polynomial proof. This was not analysed.
  • Later full test runs were much slower than the first ones. The cause was not measured.
  • The result of GitHub CI on master after the merge is unknown here.

What comes next

  • Lemmas that conclude an equality, equalities stated as two bounds, and squares of sums.
  • The rewriter's sign split, behind most random seeds that still fail in Lean.
  • Products of Real terms, the largest rejected group.
  • Reading Z3's own refutation from the hint, which needs a C++ change.

Glossary

assertion
A formula the input states as true, written (assert ...) in SMT-LIB.
atom
A piece that a theory treats as one indivisible unit. In linear arithmetic over 3x + y, the atoms are x and y.
axiom
A statement accepted without proof. A certificate that adds one is only as reliable as that statement.
bound
A comparison of a term with a constant, such as m ≥ 2. Also the name of a hint for a lemma that derives one.
certificate
A file recording the steps of a refutation so that another program can check them.
clause
A disjunction of literals, such as “not A, or B, or not C”.
clause log
The list of clauses a solver wrote while it searched, each with a note on where it came from.
cnf
Conjunctive normal form: a formula written as a conjunction of clauses. As a hint, it marks a clause that ties an assertion to that form.
cut
A lemma of integer arithmetic that removes non-integer solutions. From 2x ≥ 1 over the integers it follows that x ≥ 1.
Farkas lemma
The fact that inconsistent linear inequalities can be added up, with nonnegative multipliers, to an obvious falsehood such as 0 ≤ -1.
hint
A label on a logged clause naming the kind of reasoning that produced it, for example nla or cut.
identity
An equation that holds for every value of its variables, such as (m-2)*(n-3) = m*n - 3m - 2n + 6.
linear arithmetic
Arithmetic where variables are only added and multiplied by constants, as in 2x + 3y ≤ 7.
literal
An atomic formula or its negation, such as m ≥ 2 or not (m ≥ 2).
multiplier
The constant by which a fact is scaled before facts are added together.
nonlinear arithmetic
Arithmetic where variables are multiplied with each other, as in m * n < 6.
polynomial
A sum of products of variables and constants, such as m*n - 3m + 6.
polynomial refutation
Evidence that some facts cannot all hold. Nonnegative multiples of the facts, of products of two facts, and of squares add up to a negative constant.
product
The result of multiplying terms, called its factors. In (* m n) the factors are m and n.
query
One (check-sat) call in an SMT-LIB file, together with the assertions in force at that point.
refutation
A proof that a set of assertions leads to a contradiction.
replay
Going through a clause log again, step by step, to check each clause and rebuild the refutation.
SMT-LIB
The standard input language of SMT solvers, written with parentheses as in (assert (>= m 2)).
sorry
Lean's placeholder for a missing proof. A file that uses it is not a complete proof.
sort
The type of a value, such as Int, Real, or Bool.
tactic
A Lean command that tries to build a proof automatically. omega and grind are tactics.
term
An expression that denotes a value, such as m, (+ m 1), or (* m n).
theory lemma
A clause that is true by the rules of a theory alone, such as arithmetic, whatever the input says.
trimming
Removing from a clause log the clauses the refutation does not use.
unit propagation
When all literals of a clause but one are known false, the last one must be true. A unit clause has a single literal.
