Sweep a cnf lemma the search gives up on, gate by gate, in Lean

This pull request is open. It is not merged. The change commit is 62e88c430, the second of two commits on the branch.

In one paragraph

When the propositional proof search gives up on a cnf lemma, the reconstructor now sweeps the lemma. It sorts the gates of the original assertion and of the rewriter's form of it into equivalence classes. It then proves one small equivalence theorem per named gate and builds the refutation of the clause from them. Two QF_IDL circuit benchmarks whose certificates failed in Lean on one lemma now verify. The trust boundary is unchanged: the classes are only hints, Lean checks every declaration, and no axiom is added.

Background

Before this PR, the reconstructor tried up to three proofs of a cnf lemma. First came a search by case splits on shared atoms. Next came the same search restricted to an unsat core. Last came a fallback that unfolds every definition and calls arithmetic tactics. Two benchmarks assert one circuit of Boolean if-then-else terms each, unrolled over 20 and 30 frames. There, both searches exhausted their budget and the fallback ran Lean out of heartbeats. The rest of each file already checked. The work serves milestone 3 of the plan, equality and linear arithmetic, at larger scale.

What changed

  • Reconstructor, sweep. A cnf lemma tying two forms of one large circuit can now be proved gate by gate, as an equivalence checker compares circuits.
  • Reconstructor, classes. The classes follow the rewriter, so a nested conjunction meets its flattened form in one class.
  • Reconstructor, links. Each named gate is linked to its class's representative or canonical gate by a small theorem of its own.
  • Reconstructor, bounds. A sweep gives up early beyond fixed size bounds, and the lemma then keeps its earlier proof text.
  • Reconstructor, search. The existing search visits only its relevant gates, which made reconstruction faster without changing other outputs.
  • Tests and documentation. New tests cover classes, links, bounds, and the 20-frame circuit, and the documents describe the sweep.

An analogy: two editions of one long book. The old search quizzed both books on every combination of facts. The sweep compares them paragraph by paragraph and reuses each earlier comparison. No C++ code and no Lean library file changed.

Review

The reviewer approved the change, with no blocker and no major finding. To stress the new code, the reviewer forced the sweep on 100 sample files.

  • Minor, fixed: a malformed line. The reviewer crafted a small input whose clause the sweep refutes without any link. The reconstructor then wrote a have line that Lean rejects, and only the fallback saved the lemma. On large inputs the fallback is exactly the proof that fails. The reconstructor now records that a refutation came from the sweep, and a new test guards it.
  • Minor, fixed: no bound on the sweep's own work. Forced on a file with a thousand nested conjunctions, the sweep had not finished after 1,700 seconds. Its size limit was checked only after all the work. The sweep now counts class members before searching and characters while running, with one budget per file. The same forced file now reconstructs in seconds.
  • Nit, fixed. The plan gave the number of structures where it meant gates.
  • Nit, partly fixed: thin test coverage. The reviewer disabled parts of the sweep, and the tests that need no Lean still passed. A new test now fails when on-demand tying of gates is disabled. The second retry round is still covered only by a test in the Lean job.

One suggestion was not taken: a test holding the 30-frame circuit under the memory cap. The circuit was measured again instead.

Risks and open questions

  • A link theorem has no fallback, so one link that Lean rejected would fail the whole file.
  • The 30-frame circuit peaks at 7.14 GB of the 8 GB cap, and no test holds it there.
  • The bounds are fixed counts, not a time limit, and deep chains of conjunctions are given up on.
  • The classes cover only the rewriter rules these circuits needed. A missed rule costs links, never soundness.
  • Only two of 130 sample files reach the sweep by themselves. Other paths ran only with the searches forced off.
  • Large circuits rely on the tuple hash caching of Python 3.14.

What comes next

  • Smaller sweep text, for example congruence terms for links between gates of one shape.
  • Canonical gates nested like their class's first member, so that deep chains become linear.
  • Combining a sweep with theory clauses.
  • Sweeping before the search over the unsat core, to save reconstruction time.

Glossary

assertion
A formula the input declares to be true, written (assert ...) in SMT-LIB.
assignment
A choice of a value for every atom and variable. A theorem over all assignments holds whatever the values are.
atom
A formula with no Boolean structure inside, such as a Boolean constant p or a comparison x - y <= 3.
axiom
A statement accepted without proof. Fewer axioms mean less to trust.
Boolean
Having the value true or false. The sort of such values is written Bool.
bridge
In this project, a small theorem that an atom of the input is equivalent to Z3's normalized form of the same atom.
canonical gate
One agreed standard form chosen for all members of a class. Here it is the flat conjunction or disjunction of the members' representatives.
case split
A proof step that picks a formula and proves the goal twice, once assuming it true and once assuming it false.
certificate
A file of proof evidence that an independent checker can verify without trusting the solver.
clause
A disjunction of literals, such as (or p (not q) r).
cnf lemma
A lemma stating that a clause the solver worked with follows from the Boolean structure of an assertion. CNF, conjunctive normal form, is a conjunction of clauses.
congruence
The rule that equal or equivalent inputs give equal or equivalent outputs. Example: if a is equivalent to b, then (and a c) is equivalent to (and b c).
conjunction
An "and" of formulas. It is true when every operand is true.
declaration
A named definition or theorem in a Lean file.
disjunction
An "or" of formulas. It is true when at least one operand is true.
equivalence
The statement that two formulas always have the same truth value. Lean writes it Iff.
equivalence class
A group of formulas that are all equivalent to each other.
flattening
Rewriting nested conjunctions or disjunctions as one. Example: (and a (and b c)) becomes (and a b c).
gate
One Boolean operation inside a formula, such as a single "and", "or", or if-then-else. A formula built from gates is called a circuit.
heartbeat
Lean's unit of work for a deterministic time limit. A declaration that uses too many is rejected.
hint
Untrusted advice to the proof generator. A wrong hint can lose a proof but cannot make a false one pass.
if-then-else term
A term that picks one of two values by a condition, written (ite c a b) in SMT-LIB.
lemma
An auxiliary fact proved on the way to the main theorem.
literal
An atom or the negation of an atom.
negation
The "not" of a formula. It is true exactly when the formula is false.
proof search
An automatic procedure that tries proof steps until it finds a proof or exhausts its budget.
propositional
About Boolean structure only, treating every atom as an opaque true-or-false value.
QF_IDL
The SMT-LIB logic of quantifier-free integer difference logic. Its arithmetic atoms compare a difference with a constant, as in x - y <= 3.
refutation
A proof that a set of formulas is contradictory, which means a derivation of false from them.
representative
One member chosen to stand for its whole equivalence class.
rewriter
The solver component that simplifies terms by fixed rules. Example: (ite c false e) becomes (and (not c) e).
soundness
The property that only true statements can be proved.
sweep
A technique from circuit equivalence checking. Two circuits are compared gate by gate from the inputs upward, and each proved equivalence is reused higher up.
tactic
A Lean command that builds part of a proof automatically. Example: omega decides linear integer arithmetic goals.
theorem
A named statement together with a proof that the checker accepts.
theory clause
A clause that holds because of arithmetic or another theory, not because of Boolean structure alone.
trust boundary
The line between what must be believed without checking and what the checker verifies.
unfolding
Replacing a defined name by its definition. Lean's tactic for it is unfold.
unrolling
Copying a circuit's transition step once per time step, called a frame, to describe a bounded run.
unsat core
A subset of the formulas that is already contradictory by itself.
