Start and-elim out of a chunked conjunction from its tail theorems

Merged into master on 2026-11-16T23:10:10Z as merge commit df950b89b.

In one paragraph

This PR changes how the Lean reconstructor writes an and-elimination proof step out of a very wide conjunction. For a conjunction of more than 256 operands, the step now starts from a small theorem that Lean's kernel has already checked. Before, every proof restated a long chain of let bindings to reach its operand. On a benchmark whose single assertion has 115,040 operands, Lean ran out of heartbeats on that chain. The refutation of that benchmark now verifies in Lean. No axiom, limit, or budget changed.

Background

Since PR #139, a conjunction of more than 256 operands is defined in chunks of 32, with one tail theorem per chunk. The and-elimination step did not use them. In every proof it bound a let every 32 operands instead. Since PR #140 the QF_IDL benchmark parity/10.700.graph exported, but its main theorem failed in Lean on these lets. The plan files this PR as scale work, not as a new fragment. And-elimination is a Boolean rule of milestone 2, and the benchmark uses the linear arithmetic of milestone 3.

What changed

An analogy: every reader of a long book used to lay a bookmark every 32 pages, up to their page. Now one checked index takes each reader straight to the right section.

Reconstructor

  • An and-elimination step past the first 32 operands of a chunked conjunction now starts from its chunk's tail theorem and binds nothing.
  • The same holds when the premise depends on a hypothesis, where the old text used one projection per operand.
  • Conjunctions of at most 256 operands are written exactly as before.

Tests

  • Five new fast tests pin both forms, and in one Lean must reject two deliberately wrong proofs.
  • A new slow test checks 128,000 operands, which failed before.

Documentation

  • The plan and the README describe the new form and record the measurements.

This is the whole main theorem. The two steps that start with formula_128000_t3999 are the new feature. Each applies the last chunk's tail theorem and projects inside that chunk only. The step after them is a resolution.

Review

The verdict was approve, with no blocker, major, or minor finding. The reviewer reproduced the headline results and had Lean reject seven tampered texts.

  • Nit, a test pinned too much. A new test compared two proof lines in a fixed order that comes from Z3's clause log. A later Z3 could swap them and fail a correct proof. The test now sorts them.
  • Nit, a sentence claimed too much. The plan said the texts of all sample files were compared. Only six were written twice, and the rest are unchanged by an argument the reviewer confirmed. The sentence now says so.
  • Nit, a stale note. An older plan section still described the old behavior under hypotheses, which would mislead a reader about its cost. It now points to the new section.
  • Four suggestions. Wrong proofs were rejected only in scratch scripts. A committed test now feeds Lean two mutants, and each fails with a type mismatch. New tests also cover a full last chunk and a hypothesis inside an outlined lemma, the latter in a different form than suggested. The plan now records that eliminating every operand grows the text by under 1%.

Risks and open questions

  • Any input with an and-elimination deep in a chunked conjunction gets a different Lean text. Fast tests cover this form at several widths.
  • No proof from Z3 that was tried eliminates from a chunked conjunction under a hypothesis. Only hand-made proofs pin that path.
  • The sample files are not in the repository, and the 128,000-operand test runs only in the slow tier.

What comes next

  • Writing the Lean text of the parity files is mostly a second parse of the input. The backlog lists this first.
  • Conjunctions of 33 to 256 operands keep their lets. Lowering the threshold was not tried.
  • Three sample files still fail in Lean, for reasons outside this change.
  • The change goes upstream later in a batch, and upstream CI runs on that pull request.

Glossary

and-elimination (and-elim)
The proof rule that derives one operand from a proved conjunction. From (and a b c) it gives b.
assertion
A formula the input states as true, written (assert ...) in SMT-LIB.
atom
A formula with no logical connective inside it, such as a Boolean constant b0.
axiom
A statement accepted without proof. This PR adds none.
Boolean
The sort with the two values true and false, written Bool in SMT-LIB.
certificate
The file of proof steps exported for one refutation. The reconstructor turns it into Lean text.
chunk
In this project, a group of 32 consecutive operands of a wide conjunction, given its own Lean definition.
clause
A disjunction of literals, that is, literals joined by or. Example: (or (not a) (not b)).
clause log
The list of clauses Z3 records while solving. The pipeline replays it to build the proof.
conjunction
A formula (and a b ...) that is true when all its operands are true.
heartbeat
Lean's unit of work for one declaration. A declaration that exceeds the budget is stopped with a deterministic timeout.
hypothesis
A formula assumed inside part of a proof and discharged later.
kernel
The small trusted core of Lean that checks every proof term.
lemma
A proved statement used as a step toward a larger proof.
let binding
A local name for an intermediate result inside a proof, such as let x := ....
literal
An atomic formula or its negation, such as a or (not a).
logic (QF_IDL, QF_LIA, QF_LRA, QF_UF)
An SMT-LIB name for a fragment. QF means quantifier-free. IDL is integer difference logic, LIA and LRA are linear integer and real arithmetic, UF is uninterpreted functions.
mutant
A copy of a correct text with one deliberate error, used to test that the checker rejects it.
operand (conjunct)
One of the formulas joined by a conjunction. In (and a b) the operands are a and b.
outlined lemma
In this project, a group of proof steps moved out of the main theorem into a lemma of its own.
premise
An already proved formula that a proof step starts from.
projection
Taking one side of a proved conjunction. In Lean, h.1 is the left side and h.2 the right side.
proof object
The proof term that Z3's older core returns directly. The pipeline can use it instead of the clause log.
proof step
One application of a proof rule, with its premises and its conclusion.
refutation
A proof that the assertions together imply false.
resolution
The rule that cancels a literal in one clause against its negation in another. From (or a b) and (not a) it gives b.
sort
The type of a value, such as Int, Real, or Bool.
tail theorem
In this project, a small theorem saying that a given chunk, and everything after it, follows from the whole conjunction.
theorem
A named statement together with a proof that the kernel has checked.
type mismatch
Lean's error when a proof proves a different statement than the one claimed.
uninterpreted function
A function symbol with no fixed meaning. The solver only knows that equal arguments give equal results.
weak head normal form (whnf)
The form Lean reduces a term to when it only needs its outermost shape. The failed check ran out of heartbeats during this reduction.
