Prove the rewriter's sign split of Int products in Lean

Pull request levnach/z3#135 into master · State: MERGED · Date: 2026-11-13T22:02:06Z · Loop iteration 134

Merged on 2026-11-13T22:02:06Z as commit 5584c7175

In one paragraph

Z3's rewriter can replace a comparison of a product with zero by a formula about the signs of the factors. This sign split appears in the certificate as a rewrite step, and until now Lean could not prove it. The reconstructor now writes the proof. It factors the polynomial again and makes a case split on the sign of each factor. The linear arithmetic tactic closes every case. No axiom is added, and Lean checks every case. Of 603 unsat random inputs over Int products, 581 now verify in Lean, up from 483.

Background

Since #133 a product of terms of sort Int is an atom of linear arithmetic. Since #134 lemmas of the nonlinear solver are proved by polynomial refutations. A refutation that passed through the sign split was still exported, and then failed in Lean. The split reached the certificate as a rewrite step of an assertion, as a cnf lemma, or as a theory clause inside one. It was the largest class of Lean failures over Int products. The work serves milestone 4 of the plan, “Broader theories and integration”.

What changed

The proof uses the sign rule from school: minus times minus gives plus. Like a truth table, it walks through every combination of signs.

  • Rewrite steps. A rewrite step that splits a product comparison by the factors' signs now gets a Lean proof.
  • Cnf lemmas. The same proof covers a cnf lemma that is one theory fact, and a theory clause inside a cnf lemma.
  • Selection. Only comparisons the rewriter really split are factored, and other formulas, such as a predicate application, stay opaque atoms.
  • Fallbacks. The new proof goes first and the earlier proofs stay behind it, so certificates without Int products keep their text.
  • Tests. A new module checks documented inputs, pinned clause logs, random seeds, and one input Lean must still reject.

The change is Python only.

Review

The reviewer approved the first version with no blocker and no major finding. All four findings were fixed before the merge.

  • Minor, fixed. The reviewer asserted (or (<= (* m n) 0) (>= (* m n) 7)) with positive factors. The rewriter split the first comparison and kept the second. The kept one hid the split, so no proof was attempted and a valid input failed in Lean. The filter now also checks whether a comparison is with zero.
  • Minor, fixed. A predicate application (q m) next to a product comparison made the reconstructor give up on the whole step. Such a formula now stays an opaque atom, and the input verifies.
  • Minor, fixed. A step that equates products with zero already had a one-line proof. The new split went first and made the reviewer's file about seven times longer. Such steps now get no split.
  • Nit, fixed. No test covered a split that fails while the earlier proof succeeds. A pinned log now does.

Of three suggestions, a test through the legacy proof object was added. The other two appear as the first and third risks below.

Risks and open questions

  • Time. A failing attempt costs Lean time, and one that exhausts Lean's work budget would also block the fallback. This was not observed.
  • Size. One product of five factors takes 63 cases, and several such products could take 243.
  • Layout. One generated block relies on fixed indentation, and an error there would break the whole file.
  • Stability. The tests on documented inputs and seeds depend on how Z3 rewrites and searches. The pinned logs do not.
  • Unknown. The result of CI on master after the merge. Also, 22 of the 603 random inputs still do not verify.

What comes next

  • A factor that is an if-then-else term.
  • Equations without an integer root, such as (= (* m m) 3).
  • Cut, bound, and implied-equality steps over products.
  • More than five factors, and the two documented inputs that still fail through the legacy proof object.
  • Open items from #134, such as squares of sums and products of Real terms.

Glossary

assertion
A formula the input declares to hold, written (assert ...) in SMT-LIB.
atom
A piece that a reasoning method does not look inside. Linear arithmetic treats the product m·n as one opaque atom, like a fresh variable.
axiom
A statement accepted without proof. This project adds none.
bound
A comparison of a term with a constant, such as m ≥ 1.
case split
A proof that divides into cases covering every possibility and proves the goal in each. Example: a number is negative, zero, or positive.
certificate
A file of proof steps that an independent checker can replay to confirm a result.
clause
A disjunction of literals, such as p or not q.
clause log
The list of clauses the solver assumed and inferred, each with a hint saying why it holds.
cnf lemma
A step that ties a clause to the assertion it was derived from when the assertion was converted to clauses.
comparison
A formula that relates two terms by ≤, <, ≥, >, or =.
cut
An integer step that tightens a bound by rounding. From 2·m ≥ 1 it gives m ≥ 1.
equivalence
Two formulas are equivalent when each holds exactly if the other does.
factor
One of the terms multiplied in a product. In m·n the factors are m and n.
formula
A term of sort Bool: something that is true or false.
identity
An equation that holds for all values of its variables, such as 2·m + 3·m·n = m·(2 + 3·n).
if-then-else term
A term that picks one of two values by a condition, written (ite c a b) in SMT-LIB.
linear arithmetic
Reasoning about sums of variables multiplied by constants. Variables are never multiplied with each other.
nonlinear arithmetic
Arithmetic in which variables are multiplied with each other, as in m·n.
polynomial
A sum of products of variables with constant coefficients, such as 3·m·n − 2·m.
polynomial refutation
A proof that some facts contradict each other: multiplied and added together they give an impossible total.
predicate
A function whose result is of sort Bool. An uninterpreted predicate has a name and argument sorts but no fixed meaning.
product
The result of multiplying terms, written (* m n) in SMT-LIB.
proof object
The proof tree that Z3's older proof interface returns. It is an alternative source of certificates to the clause log.
query
One (check-sat) call in an input file, with the assertions in force at that point.
refutation
A proof that the assertions cannot all hold: it derives False from them.
rewrite step
A proof step saying that a formula and its simplified form are equivalent.
rewriter
The solver component that simplifies terms and formulas into equivalent ones before and during solving.
sign split
Deciding the sign of a product from the signs of its factors. Example: m·n ≤ 0 holds exactly if the signs of m and n differ or one of them is zero.
SMT-LIB
The standard text format for inputs to SMT solvers.
sort
The type of a value, such as Int, Real, or Bool.
tactic
A Lean command that builds part of a proof automatically. The tactic omega proves facts of linear integer arithmetic.
term
An expression that denotes a value, such as m, m + 1, or m·n.
theory clause
A clause that is true because of what the arithmetic symbols mean. Example: m < 1 or m ≥ 0.
theory lemma
A theory clause that a theory solver hands to the rest of the solver during the search.
trichotomy
The fact that every integer is less than zero, equal to zero, or greater than zero.
